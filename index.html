Cockpit & Systems

The Su-30 MKI features a tandem two-seat cockpit with a pilot up front and a Weapons System Officer in the rear. Both stations are equipped with multi-function colour displays, hands-on-throttle-and-stick controls, and helmet-mounted sighting systems.

  • ◈
    Multi-Function Displays
    Six colour MFDs across both cockpits for navigation, targeting, and systems management
  • ◉
    Helmet-Mounted Sight
    Allows off-boresight weapons cueing and targeting up to 90° off centreline
  • ⊕
    Head-Up Display
    Wide-angle HUD with forward-looking IR integration for all-weather operations
  • ▲
    HOTAS Controls
    Hands-on-throttle-and-stick for full weapons and avionics management without looking away
  • ⬡

    Zero-zero capability ejection seats for pilot safety across all flight regimes

Service History

// 1994
Contract Signed
India and Russia signed the first agreement for the Su-30 — a twin-seat version of the Su-27. The deal would evolve into one of the most comprehensive military technology transfers in history.
// 1997
MKI Prototype First Flight
The Su-30MKI prototype took to the skies with its canards, thrust-vectoring engines and advanced avionics suite — a configuration uniquely tailored for Indian Air Force requirements.
// 2002
IAF Induction Begins
The Indian Air Force formally inducted its first fully-configured Su-30MKIs at Lohegaon Air Base, Pune, marking a generational leap in India's air power projection capability.
// 2004
HAL Licensed Production
Hindustan Aeronautics Limited began licensed manufacture at Nasik, enabling India to produce Su-30MKIs domestically with increasing levels of indigenous content.
// 2019
BrahMos Integration
The Su-30MKI was successfully integrated with the BrahMos supersonic cruise missile, making it the world's only supersonic cruise missile-armed fighter aircraft of its class.
// 2024–PRESENT
Super Sukhoi Upgrade Programme
India launched the ambitious 'Super Sukhoi' programme to modernise the existing fleet with AESA radar, electronic warfare suites, indigenous weapons, and upgraded avionics — extending operational life to 2040+.
